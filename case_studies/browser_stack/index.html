Chapter 1 · The Puzzle

You clicked 10 links. How do you go back 3 steps?

Aisha has 10 tabs open: Google → Wiki → YouTube → Docs → Reddit. She needs Wiki from 3 clicks ago, but Back only undoes one step.

To reach Wiki she must go back through Reddit → Docs → YouTube one by one. With 20 pages, the first page takes 20 Backs.

👩‍🎓
AISHA
“Why can’t I just jump to Wiki? Why must I go back step by step?”
Chapter 2 · The Briefing

Back Again

Everything you visit forms a pile: every new page lands on top. Back removes the page you just saw, one at a time.

  • No jumping: you can't open a middle page directly.
  • You can't search your old pages.
  • Every new page → lands on top
  • Back → removes the top page only

Chapter 5 · The Method

How it really worked

You just lived a Stack. Here's the name for what you felt.

🥞

The Stack (LIFO)

Definition

LIFO: Last In, First Out. Like plates, you can only touch the top. Last thing you put in is first you get back.

Mechanism

push(x): visit on top, pop(): Back removes top, peek(): glance at top. All O(1). No middle jump: you must pop everything above.

Vocabulary

top: current page, underflow: Back on empty, overflow: push when full, LIFO.

If it were a Queue (FIFO)Back would give Google first, not last. Undo would erase H from HELLO → ELLO!
Because it’s a Stack (LIFO)Back gives last visited, exactly what you expect. Undo fixes last mistake.

🔑 Key Learnings

🥞
The Shape

Only the top is reachable. That constraint is the whole power, and the limit.

⚡
O(1) Top

Push, pop, peek never scan. They touch top only.

↩️
Where You'll See It

Browser Back, Undo, function calls, plate piles, coal heaps, all want the newest thing first. Printer jobs, grocery shelves and checkout lines expect the oldest first.

Chapter 6 · New Cases

Try on your own

03 · Wrong undoA text app pops the oldest letter first. You typed HELLO and undid once. What remains, and which structure is it really using: a Stack or a Queue?
04 · Fresh startYou open a fresh browser and press Back before any visit. No push has happened yet. Which case protects you, underflow or overflow, and what should the app show?
05 · Build itStart with an empty Stack. Design the push/pop sequence so Docs sits on top with Google just below it. State every operation in order.
06 · Stack or QueueA backup app undoes newest-first. The tax office must process oldest-first. Which one gets a Stack and which gets a Queue, and why?
07 · Pop orderPush A, B, C. Pop once, push D, pop twice. List the pop order, then the final Stack from bottom to top.
08 · Newest milkA shop uses a Queue so the oldest milk sells first and nothing spoils. If it switched to a Stack, which bottles would always sit at the bottom?
