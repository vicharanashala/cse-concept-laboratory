Chapter 1 · The Puzzle

Who gets their food first?

It's 8pm at a cloud kitchen. Orders flood in: Biryani → Momos → Pizza → Burger. The kitchen can cook one order at a time.

If the last order is cooked first, the family waiting for Biryani (first order) waits longest: cold food, 1-star rating.

When does first come, first served keep everyone happy, and why does any other order feel unfair?

Chapter 2 · The Briefing

Kitchen Rush

Orders form a line, you can only serve the front.

  • No cutting in, you can't lift an order from the middle.
  • VIP line is not allowed.
  • New order → joins the back (right)
  • Serve → take from the front (left) only

Chapter 5 · The Method

How it really worked

You just felt fairness. Here's the name for it.

🚶‍♂️🚶‍♀️

The Queue (FIFO)

Definition

FIFO: First In, First Out. Like a line, first to arrive is first to be served. No cutting.

Mechanism

enqueue(x): new order at rear, dequeue(): serve from front, peek(): see front. All O(1). Rear in, front out: the middle is never touched.

Vocabulary

front: next to serve, rear: last arrived, FIFO, underflow: serve when empty.

If it were a Stack (LIFO)Last order first: Biryani waits longest, gets cold
Because it’s a Queue (FIFO)First order first: everyone waits fairly, hot food

🔑 Key Learnings

🚶‍♂️
The Shape

A line with two ends, you enter at rear and leave at front.

⚖️
Fairness

Arrival order = serve order. No starvation.

🌍
Where You'll See It

Kitchen orders, printer jobs, network packets, grocery milk shelves, checkout lines, all serve the oldest first. Heaps of coal and Undo buttons serve the newest.

05 · Burger exactly thirdOrders arrive: Biryani, Momos, Pizza, Burger. Design the enqueue/dequeue sequence so Burger is served exactly third. You may serve between enqueues: how many dequeues run before Burger leaves?
