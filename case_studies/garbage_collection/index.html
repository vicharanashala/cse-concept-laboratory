Chapter 1 · The Puzzle

Her studio is full, and nobody knows why.

Zara designs posters in a little print studio app. Every poster she makes is a board, and every board can point to other boards: a poster keeps a logo board, a palette board, a gradient board. Three things are pinned up on her screen right now, the Editing now board, the Clipboard, and the Undo pile.

Feeling tidy, Zara deletes a folder of old drafts. Yet the studio's workspace never seems to shrink. New posters get made faster than space comes back, and the app starts crawling. She calls the manager: "I removed those drafts, so where is all this memory going?"

The workspace holds far more boards than the ones she can see. Only the boards you can reach by following the arrows from the pins are actually in use. Everything else sits there, taking space, with no one pointing at it at all. How can the studio tell which boards to throw away, and how should it sweep them out?

Chapter 2 · The Briefing

Follow the Arrows

Every board in the workspace is a box. Some boxes are pinned by the studio (Editing now, Clipboard, Undo pile). Arrows run from one board to another, each arrow is a reference. A board is in use only if you can walk from a pin to it, following arrows.

Your three moves

  • 👀 Inspect. Follow the arrows in your head from every pin and decide which boards they reach.
  • 🧹 Sweep. Click any board with no path from a pin to free its space.
  • 💡 Scan. Press the hint to check which boards really are still in use.

Your mission

  • 1 · Trace. For each board, find whether some pin reaches it.
  • 2 · Sweep junk. Free every board no pin can reach.
  • 3 · Clear all scenes. Finish all three studio scenes.

A board is only garbage if no pin can lead to it. Reachable means it stays.

Chapter 3 · The Studio

Chapter 4 · Case Notes

What did you notice?

No single right answer. Just what you saw in the studio.

01 · Judging a boardBefore the scan, how did you decide a board was junk? Did you count the arrows pointing at it, follow all paths from the pins, or guess from its name?
02 · The pinned chainA board far from any pin, say behind four arrows, still had to stay. What made it reachable, and what does that tell you about how far "reach" can travel?
03 · The swept boardWhen you swept a junk board, did any other board change? Its name was gone, but the boards it pointed at maybe still had other paths to them.
04 · An arrow to junkOne junk board held an arrow pointing at another junk board. Both were garbage anyway. Why didn't that arrow save either of them?
05 · Reach, not useA board a pin reaches survives even if Zara hasn't touched it in weeks. Being reachable and being actively used are different. Which one did the studio care about?
06 · Beyond the studioWhere else do things linger until nothing points at them: closed browser tabs, app filters, layers in a photo editor, your phone's "recently edited" list?
Chapter 5 · The Method

A tidy studio is quieter than a tidy desk

Your studio never "empties on purpose". It finds what can't be reached, then clears it. Here's the name.

🧹

Garbage Collection

Definition

Garbage collection is the automatic process of finding the memory a program can no longer reach from its roots and freeing it. Like the studio, what the pins can still reach stays, everything else gets swept.

Mechanism

mark: start from every root and walk all references, flagging what you touch; sweep: walk the workspace and free every unmarked object. Reachable stays, unreachable goes.

Vocabulary

root: the pins, live references the program holds, reference: an arrow to another object, reachability: a path from a root, garbage: unreachable memory, mark: tracing reachable objects, sweep: freeing the rest.

Returning memory by handYou must remember to free every board you finish. Miss one, it sits forever; free one twice, the program falls over. The workload hides in your code.
Automatic collectionJust drop the last arrow to a board. The collector traces from the pins and frees it later, in the background, and you only think about reachability.

🔑 Key Learnings

📍
Liveness Is Inherited

An object is not kept because it is important, but because some root reaches it. Delete the last path and it becomes garbage, no matter how big or young it is.

🔗
Cycles Need Tracing

Two garbage boards pointing at each other look alive if you only count incoming arrows. Mark and sweep walks from roots, so a ring with no root connection is collected anyway.

♻️
Reach, Not Intention

You cannot free a board by wishing it away; you can only drop the references to it. The collector decides the rest, which is why leaks are almost always forgotten references, not forgotten frees.

⏱️
Collection Is Deferred

Junk boards do not vanish the moment Zara stops using them. The collector runs later, which is why a program can grow before memory is quietly reclaimed.

Chapter 6 · New Cases

Spot the reachable, sweep the rest

New situations, same reachability question.

01 · App eventA music app removes a song from a playlist. If the song is still pointed at by the "now playing" queue, is it garbage, and should it be freed right away?
02 · The leakA photo viewer keeps a reference to the last 1,000 edits "just in case". Usage time never frees them. Is it the collecting that is failing, or the retained root?
03 · Two referencesA document is referenced by an open panel and by "autosave". One closes. Is the document garbage now? What is the role of the second reference?
04 · The ringThree cache boards point to each other in a circle, and nothing else points to any of them. Would a collector that only counted incoming arrows ever free them?
05 · When to collectSweeping mid slide while a user drags might freeze the frame. Some runtimes wait until memory pressure. What decides a good moment to run a collection pass?
06 · Manual freedomA library with no collector (like plain C) hands the full task to you. What kinds of mistakes does that free you to make, and does "reachable" still apply?
