Chapter 1 · The Puzzle

How do you keep a shelf neat, one jar at a time?

Meera has a box of 6 jars, all different heights. She wants them lined up on a shelf: tallest at the back, shortest at the front.

But there's a catch: she must take the jars out of the box one at a time, in the order they're packed. Each jar has to find its spot on the shelf as soon as it comes out of the box.

Say the shelf already holds 3 and 8, and jar 6 comes out next. Its spot is between them, so 8 has to shuffle one step over to make room.

Chapter 2 · The Briefing

The Neat Shelf

Jars come out of the box one at a time, in a fixed order. You slide each one into its place among the jars already on the shelf.

  • No peeking: jars come out one at a time.
  • No rebuilding: once placed, a jar stays where it is.
  • Take the next jar, find its spot, slide it in.
  • Only the neighbours that must move slide over.

Chapter 5 · The Method

How it really worked

Every time you slotted a jar into its spot, you were inserting. Here's the name for it.

🍶

Insertion Sort

Definition

Insertion Sort: build one neat stretch. Keep everything before you in order. For each new item, slide it left, shifting larger items over one at a time, until it belongs.

Mechanism

insert(x): take the next item and place it among the already-arranged items, shifting bigger ones right by one space. Repeat for every item until none are left.

Vocabulary

pass: one item handled, shift: a neighbour sliding over one space, sorted prefix: the neat part you've already built.

If you swept the line again and againYou'd keep walking the whole shelf, nudging neighbours each time, even after early jars were settled.
Because you inserted each jarOnly the newest jar moves; the neat stretch just grows, no jar is ever moved twice.

🔑 Key Learnings

🃏
The Growing Neat Part

Only the part you've handled stays neat, the box behind stays messy until its turn.

➡️
Slide, Don't Sweep

A new item shifts only the neighbours that must move, nothing else in the line is touched.

🌍
Where You'll See It

Cards in a hand, books you slot onto a shelf, paper slips into a stack, all insert one at a time. Great when the pile is already nearly neat.

Chapter 6 · New Cases

Try on your own

Work it out in your head.

01 · Count the shiftsShelf holds 5 and 7. Jar 4 arrives. How many jars shift right on this insertion pass, and where does 4 land?
02 · The easy orderJars arrive 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 in perfect height order. How many shifts does the whole round need, and why is it so small?
03 · The reverse pileJars arrive biggest first: 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3. Which single pass makes the most shifts, and roughly how many shifts in total?
04 · Re-sort each timeAn assistant empties and re-sorts the whole shelf after every jar. Why does that move already placed jars again, and how does a single insertion pass avoid it?
05 · Use the prefixThe sorted prefix is 1, 3, 6, 8 and jar 9 arrives last. How many shifts does the final insertion pass need, and where does 9 land?
06 · Pick the boxTwo boxes: one arrives nearly sorted, the other fully reversed. Which box makes insertion sort shine, and which forces the most shifts?
07 · Marksheet stackA teacher files marksheets by roll number, sliding only the sheets after each new one. When is this fastest, when the stack is nearly sorted or fully shuffled? Why?
