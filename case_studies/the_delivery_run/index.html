Chapter 1 · The Puzzle

Five drops, no map, just a chain of notes.

Meera delivers parcels across town on her scooter. Her route isn't a map. It's a stack of paper notes clipped to her handlebar. Each note has one drop on it, and at the bottom, a line that says which note comes next. The first note is where she starts; the last note just says "done" and points at nothing.

Halfway through the run, a customer texts: "add my flat upstairs, it's on the way", a new stop that belongs somewhere in the middle of the chain. Another texts: "nobody's home, skip me", a stop that has to come out.

There's no numbered street list to re-clip. Just the chain of notes, one pointing to the next. How does Meera change the route without losing her place or the stops behind her?

Chapter 2 · The Briefing

One Link at a Time

You hold one note at a time. That note shows a drop, and at its bottom one line: which note comes next. Each note is only ever connected to the one after it. That is the whole route.

Your three moves

  • ➡️ Move to the next note. Put down the note you hold and pick up the one it points to.
  • ➕ Add a delivery. Clip a new note in right after the one you hold.
  • ✖ Cancel the next stop. Take out the note right after yours.

Your mission

  • 1 · Walk the chain. Start at the first note and keep moving forward until you reach the last note.
  • 2 · Add one delivery. Press ➕ while stopped at any note.
  • 3 · Cancel one stop. Press ✖ to drop the note right after yours.
Add = 2 rewritesCancel = 1 rewriteMove = free

Do all three jobs with 3 rewrites or fewer.

Chapter 4 · Case Notes

What did you notice?

No single right answer. Just what you saw on the route.

01 · No jumpingStanding at the start note, could you peek at the 4th note without passing notes 2 and 3? What did you actually have to do to get there?
02 · The addWhen you clipped the new note into the middle, which notes' "next" lines had to change? How many, and did any other note notice?
03 · The cancelWhen you pulled a note out of the middle, where did the note after it go? Did the chain break, or did somebody start pointing at it instead?
04 · The end noteThe last note's "next" line pointed at nothing. What would go haywire if it pointed at some random note instead?
05 · Same run, twiceThe three jobs, move, add and cancel, each took a different amount of line-rewriting. Which felt local (only nearby notes changed) and which forced you to walk?
06 · Beyond the runWhere else do things only ever point to "what comes next": playlists, chat threads, your browser's back button?
Chapter 5 · The Method

How it really worked

You walked a chain where each note only knows the next one. Here's the name.

🔗

Linked List

Definition

A linked list is a chain of stops where each stop stores its own data plus a link to the next stop. You can only reach a stop by following links from the start.

In the game, each note was one stop: it held the drop's name and a line pointing to the next note. Moving was traversal. Adding a delivery inserted a stop by rewiring two lines. Cancelling deleted a stop by rewiring one line. The Head is the entry point; the Tail's link is empty.

Mechanism

traverse: from the Head, follow next until you reach the stop you want (or the Tail). insert after stop P: point the new stop at P's old next, then point P at the new stop, 2 rewrites. delete the stop after P: point P directly at that stop's next, 1 rewrite. Nothing else in the chain ever changes.

Vocabulary

node: one stop (data + link), head: the first node, entry point, tail: the last node, its link is null, next: the link to the following node, traversal: walking the chain node by node, insertion / deletion: local rewiring of links, O(n): reaching the k-th node takes up to k steps.

One long numbered listSlip a new stop in at position 3 and every stop after it gets renumbered, up to n shifts for one add.
A chain of notesAdd or cancel with 2 (or 1) line rewrites, the rest of the route never notices, wherever the change lands.

🔑 Key Learnings

🚶
No Jumping, Only Walking

Each stop knows only where the next one is. Finding the 5th stop still costs 5 follows from the Head, distance on the chain is what costs, not physical distance on the map.

✂️
Changes Are Local

Insert rewrites 2 links, delete rewrites 1, no matter how long the chain is, and no stop after the change has to move or renumber.

🧩
Where You'll See It

Playlists (play-next splices a song in), chat and undo history, your browser's back button, queues at a counter: anything that grows, shrinks, or changes in the middle often and rarely needs instant jump-to-number-500 access.

Chapter 6 · New Cases

Try on your own

Work it out in your head.

01 · Long walkYou're holding note 2 of 6 and the new delivery belongs after note 5. How many traversal steps come first, and which two next links get rewritten at the end?
02 · Cancelling the HeadWhat if the first note itself is cancelled: which note becomes the new Head? And what if there was only one note to begin with?
03 · Cutting aheadYou want the new delivery to be dropped off first, before the current Head. Which single link change does it, and what does the new note point at?
04 · Hundred-stop runA chain has 100 stops and you need the very last one. Worst case, how many follows? Now: how many follows does it take to cancel stop 50, once you're standing at stop 49?
05 · The circleImagine the Tail's line pointed all the way back to the Head instead of nowhere. What new trouble could a careless "Move Next" run into?
06 · Two ways to storeA numbered street list can jump straight to stop 50 but renumbers everything on every add. A chain never renumbers but always walks. Which would Meera prefer, and when would the other one win?
